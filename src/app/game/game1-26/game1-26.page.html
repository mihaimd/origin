~ 🧜‍♀️ The Song of the Deep ~

You reach out toward one of the shimmering bubbles, feeling the water hum around your fingertips. As your hand passes through its glowing surface, a question forms—not in words, but as a memory surfacing from beneath the waves.

You close your eyes and speak your answer aloud, steady and sure, though you don’t know how you know it.

For a moment, silence falls across the lake. The mists pause. The air grows heavy.

Then the bubble bursts into radiant light, and the water begins to shimmer around you like a curtain parting.

The mermaid rises from the lake again, her eyes wide with something between curiosity and recognition.

“Few among your kind remember such things,” she says softly. “Yet the knowledge sleeps within you. That is no accident.”

She lifts her hands, and the skyroot fronds drift toward you, glowing faintly with silver light. You gather them carefully, their scent sharp and sweet like crushed mint and riverstone.

Then, without another word, she offers you a small, radiant shard—iridescent like a pearl and etched with delicate lines that seem to mirror the pendant’s mysterious symbols.

“This belonged to a song long forgotten,” she says. “A verse broken apart when the Veil was raised. It may find its voice again, through you.”

You tuck the shard into your satchel, its warmth pulsing faintly beside the pendant and the other strange relics you’ve gathered. Your journey is beginning to feel less like chance and more like fate.

Just as you begin to turn away, the mermaid lifts a finger, her expression turning solemn.

“There is one more gift, should you have the courage.”

She beckons toward a cluster of tangled green weeds floating at the water’s edge, their leaves veined with silver.

“This is deepgill weed,” she says. “If chewed, it will grant you breath beneath the water—for a short time. Enough, perhaps, to reach the cave below.”

You blink. “There’s a cave?”

She nods slowly. “It sleeps in the heart of the lake—hidden in the silt, forgotten by most. But something waits there. Something lost. A treasure, yes—but not only gold. A fragment of memory.”

You stare into the dark waters, heart racing with the thrill of discovery.

“When you are ready,” she says, “eat the weed and dive. But beware—the lake remembers not only joy.”

With that, she sinks below the surface once more, her form dissolving into liquid silver. Only the deepgill weed remains, gently swaying with the tide.

You gather the fronds, the shard, and the strange weed, and step back from the shore—your satchel heavier, your mind spinning with wonder.

The lake is not finished with you. Not yet.
