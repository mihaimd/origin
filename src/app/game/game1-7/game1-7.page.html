~ The Smith of Coins and Questions ~

The sun has risen higher now, casting long shadows through the awnings. The market is alive with color and barter: laughter, clinking copper, the neigh of a restless mule tethered near the stables.

You pass baskets of fire peppers, hanging herbs drying in the sun, and a girl selling feather charms that twitch in your presence.

But then you see him—old man seated behind a table of nothing but coins.

No produce. No cloth. No wares at all.

Just coins—dozens of them, each one different. Ancient. Bent. Faintly glowing with the green-blue gleam of old metal.

You feel the pendant stir.

- “Pick one,” the old man says without looking up. “Only one. But it must be the right one.”

You pause. “The right one for what?”

He finally looks up, and his eyes are white—not blind, but blank, like stone rubbed smooth by time.
